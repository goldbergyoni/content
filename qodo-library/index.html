Three wireframe options

All three cover the same brief — W1 to W6 plus the overlap and un-adopt moments — and all three use the unchanged design system from the Standards vision prototype: same shell, same tabs, same card and star-to-adopt pattern, same toast copy, same palette and type. What differs is the information architecture: where the catalog lives, where the proof lives, and what you are looking at when you decide.

Content is real. Rule titles come from the mined Node.js, frontend-testing and SDLC rule sets, and the example findings link to actual pull requests from the battle-testing runs.

Every option answers the same screens
W1 Library section W2 Package detail — the trust screen W3 Library rules in the Rules table W4 Package impact W5 Update visibility W6 Observe mode Related rules found Un-adopt confirmation
Open any file and use the Wireframe states panel in the bottom-right corner to jump straight to a named state. Everything is clickable — stars adopt, toggles turn single rules off, findings link out to the real pull requests.

A

Card grid + detail page the existing version

The wireframe that already exists, drawn out and extended with exactly the two things W1 asks for: a Recommended for you marker with its reason in one phrase, and a way into the package before adopting. The Library stays a card grid on the Company standards tab; the trust screen, impact and changelog live on a full page you navigate to and come back from.

Why it holds up
  • Zero new shell — it is the prototype plus two affordances
  • Cards read well at a catalog of three to six packages
  • The detail page has room for evidence, pipeline, impact and changelog without cramming
  • Cheapest to build and lowest risk of the three
What it costs
  • Deciding between two packages means navigating back and forth
  • The proof is one click away from the decision, not next to it
  • The card grid stops scaling once the catalog grows past a screen
  • Leads with the catalog, so it never tells you what you are missing

B

Split catalog — list left, evidence docked right

One screen, two panes. The catalog never leaves the left rail; the package's trust panel is docked on the right with sub-tabs for Rules, Evidence, Impact and Changelog. Choosing between packages and checking our work become the same motion — click a different package and the proof swaps under you. No navigation, no back button, no losing your place.

Why it is better
  • The decision and the evidence are never separated
  • Comparing packages is one click, not a round trip
  • Filters (recommended / adopted / available) make the catalog scale past a card grid
  • Per-rule toggles sit next to the hit counts that justify them
What it costs
  • Needs a fixed-height, internally scrolling layout — new for this tab
  • The right pane gets narrow on smaller screens
  • Still catalog-first: it answers "is this good" better than "what am I missing"

C

Blind-spot map — adopt after a dry run on your own code

Leads with the problem the PRD actually names: the gap is invisible from inside, and nothing tells you what you are not checking. The entrance is a coverage matrix — your detected stack down the side, concerns across the top, blind spots drawn as dashed empty cells. Packages are ranked by how much of your map they close, and every one is presented with a dry run: what it would have caught on your own merged pull requests, before you commit to anything.

Why it is better
  • Creates the demand instead of waiting to be browsed — the empty cells do the selling
  • Adoption is backed by your history, not only our benchmark repos
  • Observe mode becomes a first-class adoption choice, not a phase-2 setting buried in a header
  • Un-adopting shows exactly which cells go dark again
  • The same matrix, re-coloured, answers "was it worth it"
What it costs
  • Depends on stack detection and a dry-run capability — the most backend work of the three
  • A matrix of your own rules implies a category taxonomy the product does not have yet
  • Furthest from the existing prototype, so the most to review with design
